Introduction

Learning to move noise into images

Flow matching learns a time-dependent velocity field that transports samples from a simple noise distribution toward the image distribution. This project studies that idea at two scales: first by probing and steering a pretrained text-conditioned model, then by implementing and training compact UNets on MNIST. Together, the experiments show how interpolation, conditioning, classifier-free guidance, and frequency-aware composition shape the generated result.

Core idea

A straight probability path

Given clean data x1 and Gaussian noise x0, flow matching trains a vector field along the straight interpolation between them. At inference time, Euler integration follows the learned velocity from noise at t=0 to data at t=1.

xt = (1 − t)x0 + tx1
utarget = x1 − x0
L = E ||uθ(xt, t) − utarget||²

Part A

The Power of Flow Models

The required Campanile experiments are paired with Minecraft scenes whose block structure, architecture, creatures, and sharp silhouettes make preservation and semantic drift easy to see.

A.1.1–A.1.2 · Forward process and classical denoising

What Gaussian blur cannot recover

seed 180

I sample one Gaussian endpoint and reuse it for every displayed value of t. This makes the interpolation direction fixed: only the clean-image weight changes. For the classical baseline, I first undo the clean-image attenuation by dividing by t, then sweep Gaussian σ ∈ {1,2,4,8,16} and report the lowest-MSE result.

Observation. The MSE-optimal blur grows wider as corruption increases, yet visual fidelity decreases. A low-pass filter only removes energy; a generative velocity field can supply a learned image prior.
A.1.3 · One-step denoising

One velocity estimate to the endpoint

a high quality photo

A single network evaluation extrapolates the noisy state to t=1. Performance improves with larger t, because the input contains more evidence about the intended image.

x̂1 = xt + (1 − t)vθ(xt, t, c)
A.1.4 · Velocity diagnostics

What the model adds

shared RGB scale

Because both the clean image and original noise are known, the exact path velocity is available. I visualize endpoint updates rather than raw velocities and compute RMS error across RGB at each pixel before display scaling.

vgt = x1 − ε   ·   Et = √meanRGB(((1−t)(v̂t−vgt))²)
A.1.5 · Iterative denoising

Re-evaluating the field matters

25 Euler updates

Starting from the same saved t=0.50 input, Euler’s method recomputes velocity after every h=0.02 step. The implementation shortens the last step when necessary so the final state lands exactly at t=1.

xt+h = xt + h · vθ(xt, t, c)
A.1.6–A.2.1 · Sampling and classifier-free guidance

Guidance sharpens the generic prompt

w=7 · h=0.02

Five matched seeds isolate the effect of CFG. Both branches evaluate the same state and time; setting w=0 is asserted to reproduce ordinary conditional prediction exactly.

vcfg = vcond + w(vcond − vuncond)
A.3.1 · Image-to-image projection

Starting time controls fidelity

7 required t values

Each sequence uses tstart ∈ {0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.10, 0.14, 0.25, 0.50}. Small values allow semantic reinvention; large values preserve composition and block layout. The official Campanile and two Minecraft scenes receive the complete sweep.

Downloaded image and drawing-style inputs

The two simple Minecraft drawings use broad shapes and sparse outlines, matching the instructor-approved handwritten-style requirement.

Earlier material failures

These two diagnostic sweeps are retained at half width. The Aether scene drifts toward unrelated natural-image objects at low t, while the humanoid Warden repeatedly triggers portrait-like generations. Both failures motivated the architecture-focused Frosted Prison replacement.

Replacing the weak Warden material

Fourteen official Cataclysm screenshots were ranked by subject size, symmetry, contrast, and likelihood of avoiding portrait bias. Frosted Prison gave the cleanest structure-preserving result. A matched sweep with seed 12 confirms that t=0.14 gives the strongest balance between a recognizable source layout and a coherent photorealistic edit.

A.3.3 · Visual anagrams

One image, two orientations

180° transform

At every Euler step, the first prompt predicts velocity upright. The state is rotated, evaluated under the second prompt, and rotated back before averaging.

v(x,t) = ½[vcfg(x,t,c₁) + R(vcfg(R(x),t,c₂))]

Why this replacement worked. “Village / horse” shares a long horizontal mass and high-contrast outer contour. The earlier “fortress / fire dragon” pair required incompatible vertical and winged silhouettes.

A.3.4 · Hybrid images

Near detail, distant structure

frequency-composed velocity

The distant prompt supplies low-frequency velocity while the close prompt supplies high-frequency residuals. Downsampling removes fine residuals, allowing the coarse subject to dominate.

v = lowpass(vdistant) + vclose − lowpass(vclose)
Parameter selection. σ=12 preserves castle edge detail for the close view while assigning a broad silhouette to the dragon. σ=16 and 24 made the far subject stronger but obscured the near interpretation.
Part B

Flow Matching from Scratch

Only the official MNIST training split is used. The experiments move from an unconditional one-step denoiser to time conditioning, then add one-hot class conditioning, conditional dropout, and classifier-free guidance.

B.1.1 · Implementing the UNet

A prescribed convolutional UNet

D=128 · 5 epochs

The network follows the handout’s atomic operations and skip topology: two downsampling stages, a 1×1 bottleneck, two transposed-convolution upsampling stages, and a final single-channel prediction.

Conv = Conv2d → BatchNorm → GELU
Down = stride-2 Conv + ConvBlock
Up = stride-2 ConvTranspose + ConvBlock

B.1.2.1 · Training at σ=0.5

Fresh ε is sampled after every batch is fetched. Adam uses 10−4. Microbatch 64 with four-step accumulation preserves effective batch 256 on the 2 GB GPU.

B.1.2.2 · Out-of-distribution noise

B.1.2.3 · Why one-step generation fails

A second UNet receives independent Gaussian noise paired with random clean digits. The input contains no target identity, so MSE rewards the conditional mean rather than diverse samples.

B.2.1–B.2.3 · Time-conditioned flow matching

Learning velocity along the whole path

D=64 · 50 steps

B.2.1 · Adding time conditioning

For every batch, x0~N(0,I) and continuous t~Uniform[0,1] are resampled. Two FCBlocks map scalar time into channel-wise features. Training predicts x1−x0; sampling integrates from fresh noise.

B.2.2 · Training the time-conditioned UNet

The epoch-mean MSE falls from 0.1650 to 0.09181 over ten full passes through the 60,000-image training split.

B.2.3 · Sampling from the time-conditioned UNet

B.2.4–B.2.6 · Class conditioning and CFG

Controllable generation

puncond=0.1 · γ=5

B.2.4 · Adding class conditioning

A one-hot digit vector receives two FC embeddings parallel to time. During training, 10% are zeroed so one network learns conditional and unconditional velocities. Sampling combines them with CFG.

uCFG = uuncond + γ(ucond − uuncond)

B.2.5 · Training the class-conditioned UNet

The epoch-mean MSE decreases from 0.1572 at epoch 1 to 0.08471 at epoch 10. Conditioning makes the fixed-seed grid fully class-legible by epoch 5, while later epochs refine stroke consistency.

Required sampling protocol

Four instances of every digit 0–9 begin from fixed seed 180 and use 300 Euler steps. Changes across epochs therefore reflect learning, not new initialization.

Adam starts at 10−2; ExponentialLR multiplies by 0.11/10 after each epoch.

B.2.6 · Sampling with classifier-free guidance

Required ablation · removing the scheduler

Holding 10−2 constant is unstable. I compensate by using fixed 10−3 and extending training from 10 to 15 epochs, trading fast early movement for stable late optimization.

Ablation conclusion. The lower constant rate reaches 0.08357 final epoch-mean MSE versus 0.08471 with the scheduler and produces comparably legible classes, but needs five extra epochs. The scheduler is not essential for quality here, but is more compute-efficient.

Conclusion

What the experiments show

Part A + Part B

Across both parts, flow matching works best when the model receives enough structure to predict a coherent direction without being forced to invent incompatible semantics. The pretrained experiments expose that tradeoff visually; the MNIST experiments reproduce it from first principles.

Structure precedes detail

For image-to-image editing, larger starting times preserve composition and voxel geometry, while smaller values let the learned prior dominate. Architectural subjects survive this transition more reliably than humanoid game characters.

Guidance needs a compatible prompt

CFG strengthens semantic direction, but it cannot repair a poor match between source geometry and text. Prompts describing material, silhouette, environment, and photographic style were more reliable than mod-specific names.

Iterative flow enables generation

A one-step pure-noise denoiser collapses toward an average digit. Time conditioning makes iterative transport possible; class conditioning adds control, while the scheduler mainly improves compute efficiency rather than final attainable quality.

Overall. Flow matching is more than noise removal: it learns a direction field over an entire probability path. Starting time, conditioning, guidance, and numerical integration determine how faithfully that field follows source structure or moves toward a requested concept.
